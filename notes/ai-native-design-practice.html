AI-native design practice

How I design when AI is part of the medium, the interface, and the making process. At the center of it is co-creation as distribution of agency: deciding at each particular moment who takes the next step, and how the results come back and get acted upon. This works on all levels of scale: my own process, things I build, team collaboration, society at large. This is also how new forms of work (learning, making, play) appear and old ones get redefined.

My own desk & practice

When talking about AI, the topic of speed tends to take over. But design intrinsically requires hands in the clay. The challenge then is to maintain control over the design direction while leveraging AI for operational efficiency. So, some examples of who is doing what:

  • 🧑 Successful delegation to 🤖 depends on context, so I'm trying to capture reusable sequences and tacit knowledge as something that can be passed to an agent.1
  • 🧑 Design is happening either in prose or directly in code. Ok, there is always pen & paper as well. Figma is mostly out.
  • 🧑 Focusing more on information architecture as a way to ground generation in design situation's context.
  • 🧑 Following the research relevant to my work.
  • 🧑 Learning to pick my battles.
  • 🤖 Expands my capacity to take in information by making data more accessible, doing secondary research.
  • 🤖 Translates: generates prototypes from specifications, drafts design artifacts (e.g. plans from briefs), adjusts reports for target audience (e.g. business stakeholders or domain experts)
  • 🤖 Monitoring alignment between different parts of the design.
  • 🤖 Helps notice patterns and make connections.
  • 🤖 Enables reflective dialogue into my process to challenge assumptions, critique ideas and provoke divergent thinking.
  • 🤖 Admin: fetching/pushing information from collaboration software, keeping track of teams' progress, pushing/pulling changes to the design system, etc.
  • 🤖 Thinking about metrics (it should be me, but I'm bad at it)
What remains the same? Just in case – design process is still there, human-centered and keeps its overall shape. Strategy gets more attention, user research gets done.
This is also a refactoring opportunity: existing ways of working are shaped by both the technology and the human constraints.

Made things (a.k.a. products)

For the thing to co-create with users, it needs to be aware of the context and meaning of the experience it is made to support.

  • The design's target drifts from the product to capability that depending on circumstances can be delivered by an app, but can also be an interaction of one or several apps, agents (human and non-human), documents, etc.
  • Generative UI...
What remains the same? Orientation toward isolated products always fitted badly with users who live in a fluid ecology of hundreds of apps, platforms, infrastructures and other people's content.

Team & Organisation

Co-creation with AI reshapes roles and responsibilities within teams and organisations. As a result, there is an ongoing change in the way people work together. Different teams adopt differently but here are some 🧩 patterns I see in my  work:

  • Myself as a team member: I like to do design, development, and product. I like doing all of these things. I see a growing need for service design language and tooling in the work I do. I also always wanted to do more things around information architecture as something that informs both design and development and now I do. Is my role still a UX/product designer? If it means doing whatever needs to be done to make the thing better, then yes.
  • Me & other designers: everyone I know does this differently. The common thread is that we are all trying to find a balance between human and AI contributions, and to make sure that the end result makes things better.
  • Me & engineering (and their 🤖): working on either the same artifacts or having a protocol for translation1.
  • Me & product managers (and their 🤖): the boundary between design and product management was always fuzzy and negotiated within each specific configuration of people and context. So, in a way, nothing has changed.
A typical shape is code-level collaboration and spec-driven workflows. First, depending on complexity, some of the solutions I can implement myself. Second, the shape of the handoff when there is one is different. Even if implementation complexity is beyond my skill as developer, I can still implement a prototype in a real codebase. Also, if a team is practicing some form of spec-driven-development, the handoff is a specification.
What remains the same? Close collaboration between users, designers, developers and product managers (those can be hats, not necessarily different people) is still the most effective way to build software.
